# Squeeze Watch — Methodology

## What this page is

Squeeze Watch is a nightly screen of publicly traded names where two
characteristics are showing up together on the same evening:

1. A meaningful share of float reported short.
2. Active options interest on the tape.

It is published once per day, after the close, at 5 PM ET. It is not a
live feed and it is not a watchlist of names to trade.

The page is part of a trader's journal. It records what a screen turned
up tonight, with short observations about the names at the top of the
list. It does not project, advise, or predict.

## What "short pressure" means

Two complementary readings are commonly used:

- **Short interest as a share of float.** This is the number of shares
  reported short divided by shares available to trade. When this number
  is high, a larger proportion of the tradable supply is positioned for
  a price decline. The reading is reported on a delay by exchanges and
  is therefore a description of recent positioning, not of today's
  positioning.

- **Days to cover.** This is the reported short interest divided by the
  recent average daily share volume. It is a rough measure of how
  compressed the short position is — how many trading days of normal
  volume it would take, on paper, to unwind the position.

Neither number is a prediction. Both have well-known limitations:
short-interest data lags, share-locating practices vary, and average
volume can swing on a single newsworthy session.

## Why we look at options activity alongside short pressure

Options interest sits next to the short-pressure numbers on this page
because the combination of the two is often the part that's most
informative. A high short interest reading on its own can sit there for
months without anything else changing. When options activity picks up
on the same name, it suggests other market participants are
re-positioning — which doesn't predict an outcome, but does make the
combination more notable than either reading would be alone.

The page reports the ratio of call-side to put-side options interest as
observed tonight, and a count of the alerts that fired on the name. The
ratio and the count are descriptions of tape conditions; neither is a
direction call.

## Why some names are filtered out

Before scoring, the screen drops any name that does not clear a
liquidity floor. The intent is to exclude micro-caps and very thinly
traded names where short-interest data, options activity, and price
moves can all be driven by a handful of trades rather than by anything
durable. The page only evaluates names with a reasonable level of
market capitalisation and average daily share volume.

## What the bands mean

Names are grouped into three descriptive bands:

- **Elevated** — both short-pressure readings and options interest were
  high relative to other names in the screen tonight.
- **Notable** — one reading was high while the other was meaningful.
- **Watching** — the combination registered enough to keep an eye on,
  without standing out.

The bands are descriptive labels for tonight's screen. They are not
ratings, and they are not direction calls.

## What this page deliberately does not do

- It does not recommend, advise, or suggest action on any name listed.
- It does not project the size, direction, or timing of any future
  price move.
- It does not stream live data. The list is a single end-of-day
  snapshot.
- It does not disclose the exact thresholds used to score names, nor
  the data sources behind the readings.

If you find yourself reading this page as a list of names to buy or
sell, please re-read this section. The page is a description of
conditions, not a list of trades.

## Cadence

Published once each weekday at 5 PM ET. The "as of" stamp on the page
is the snapshot time, not the time you opened the browser. If a feed
upstream of the page degrades, the page shows a data-quality note and
the previous evening's snapshot remains visible.

## References

- Public exchange short-interest reporting (FINRA twice-monthly
  filings) is the standard source for short-interest data; values are
  reported on a delay.
- Options chain and flow data is sourced through institutional-grade
  vendors and is published once per evening only.
